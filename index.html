      How to read this

      • Bit depth is checked sample by sample: if the lowest bits are always zero, a "24-bit" file is really 16-bit.
      • Cutoff is a sharp wall in the spectrum. Measured with LAME 3.100 at 44.1 kHz: 128 kbps ≈ 17.0 kHz, 192 kbps/V2 ≈ 19.2, 256 kbps ≈ 20.0, 320 kbps ≈ 20.4–20.7. LAME V0, AAC 256 and Vorbis q6 kept full bandwidth. Opus stops at 20 kHz.
      • Hi-res (88.2 kHz and up) should have content above 24 kHz. A wall near 22 or 24 kHz means it was upsampled from CD or 48 kHz.
      • On real music (lossy copies of a commercial CD-quality track), MP3 at 128–320 kbps, LAME V0/V2 and Opus sources were all caught; real-music cutoffs ran about 0.3–0.5 kHz lower than on test signals, so the bitrate shown is an estimate. AAC 256 and Vorbis q6 sources were not caught.
      • Bitrate is measured from the audio data itself (tags and cover art excluded; every MP3 frame is read). For lossy files it is compared with the cutoff: a 320 kbps MP3 that stops at 17 kHz was upconverted from about 128 kbps. Expected cutoffs were measured on a real track: MP3 128 ≈ 17.0, 160 ≈ 17.8, 192 ≈ 19.2, 256 ≈ 19.9, 320 ≈ 20.6 kHz; ffmpeg AAC 128 ≈ 17.7 and full band from 256; Opus always 20 kHz. Other AAC encoders (Apple, FDK) may differ, so AAC results are marked medium confidence.
      • Defective means the length stored in the header (FLAC, WAV, AIFF, MP3 with a Xing/Info header) doesn't match the audio that actually decodes, which happens with truncated or damaged downloads. A file the browser normally plays but can't decode is marked Unreadable.
      • Codec frame grid re-analyses the audio with the 2048-sample MDCT that AAC and Vorbis use, at all 1024 frame offsets. Audio decoded from those codecs shows a sharp peak of quantized-away coefficients at one offset; genuine audio scores about 1.0–1.3. In tests on real music it caught ffmpeg AAC 128–320 (scores 5–22) and Vorbis q6–q10 (2.4–3.7) with no false alarms on 13 genuine tracks. It does not detect MP3 (LAME V0 stayed invisible in dense music) and was not tested with Apple or FDK AAC. It takes about 20–60 seconds per file.
      • A low-pass between 19.5 and 20.9 kHz with a gentle slope (wider than 800 Hz from −3 to −30 dB, and less than 32 dB deep) is treated as a likely mastering filter, not proof of a lossy source; lossy encoders measured here cut within 150–620 Hz.
      • Spectral holes and band gating are supporting fingerprints of lossy codecs, tuned on test signals rather than on a large music library, so treat them as hints.
      • A 20 kHz wall can also come from mastering. "Genuine" means no evidence against the file was found, not proof that it is clean.
      • Quality score (0–100) rates how genuine and clean a file is. Authenticity is 40 points (a real lossless file that matches its label gets all 40). Cleanliness is 40: no clipping runs, true peak (full marks at −1 dBTP or lower, none at +2), no damage or truncation, no DC offset, full bandwidth. Proven 24-bit is 10, awarded only when the quietest part of the track sits at least 10 dB below what 16-bit can represent; a file that is really 24-bit but never gets that quiet is not penalised, it simply can't earn these points. Dynamics (PLR and LRA) is 10. The weights are this tool's own choice, not an industry standard.
      • Vinyl check looks for the low-frequency fingerprint of a record: warps move the stylus up and down, a stereo cartridge turns that into an out-of-phase (side) signal, and the tonearm resonance (about 8–15 Hz) boosts it. Digital masters keep content below 20 Hz mono and tiny. "Likely" needs a side-channel hump that is at least as strong as the mid channel there. It was tuned on a small set of files, so treat it as a strong hint, not proof; a rip with a strong subsonic filter can hide it. It does not change the quality score.
      • Mastering numbers describe the production, not the file's honesty. PLR is true peak minus integrated loudness and PSR is true peak minus the loudest 3-second moment; low values (under about 9 dB) mean heavy limiting. Tonal balance is the whole-track level of each octave relative to the 1 kHz octave. Streaming checks use Spotify's published guidance: normalisation to −14 LUFS, true peak at or below −1 dBTP, or −2 dBTP for masters louder than −14 LUFS (Spotify for Artists). LRA follows EBU Tech 3342.
      • Loudness follows ITU-R BS.1770 / EBU R128 (integrated LUFS, LRA). True peak uses 4× oversampling. Checked against ffmpeg: loudness within 0.1 LU, true peak within about 0.2 dB for lossless files. For MP3 and other lossy files the browser's own decoder can flatten overs, so true peak may read up to about 1 dB low.

      Sources: LAME info tag layout · LAME VBR method codes · LAME V0 lowpass (Hydrogenaudio). Cutoff figures are this tool's own measurements.
